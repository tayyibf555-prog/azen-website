# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

UK small-to-mid business owners and operations leads (logistics 3PLs, accounting firms, tuition centres, nurseries, community organisations — the seven live case studies define the lane). They arrive from a cold email, a referral, or a reel, usually outside office hours, and decide inside ninety seconds whether Azen is a real engineering shop or another AI-hype agency. They are budget-conscious, burned by agencies before, and allergic to jargon. The job to be done: "convince me you can remove my manual bottlenecks without selling me something generic, then let me book a call."

## Product Purpose

azen.io is the marketing surface for Azen, a London AI-infrastructure agency founded by Tayyib Arbab. Azen maps a client's exact bottlenecks and deploys custom AI systems (voice agents, CRMs, automation pipelines) on flat fees. The site exists to earn engineering credibility fast and convert it into a booked Calendly audit. Success = qualified audits booked; the page is a filter as much as a funnel — it should repel bargain hunters wanting templates and attract owners who value custom work.

## Brand Personality

Precise, candid, load-bearing. The voice of a senior engineer explaining what they'd build and why, not a marketer promising magic. Quiet confidence, zero hype: claims are specific (hours automated, systems shipped), promises are scoped, and the one emotional note allowed is the relief of leverage — "stop renting time." Design should feel engineered: everything aligned, everything earning its place, nothing decorative that pretends to be functional.

## Anti-references

- Neon-gradient "AI magic" agency sites: purple-to-cyan blobs, sparkles emoji, GPT-wrapper energy.
- Template SaaS landing pages: hero-metric blocks, identical icon-card grids, stock 3D illustrations.
- The Stripe/Linear/Vercel clone lane (Inter, purple-on-black minimal). The site deliberately chose Hanken Grotesk over Inter/Mona to stay out of it.
- The founder's own words, on the site itself: "Most agencies sell you overpriced solutions you don't need." The site must never feel like those agencies.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** An agency selling engineered systems must ship an engineered site: real numbers, real proof surfaces, honest motion. Nothing faked, nothing decorative masquerading as data.
2. **Evidence over adjectives.** Every claim is paired with proof: case studies with named clients, operational stats, the founder on camera. If a section makes a claim without evidence, add evidence or cut the claim.
3. **Two weights, one voice.** The thin/bold couplet ("Stop renting time. / **Build leverage.**") is the visual signature. Typography carries the brand; imagery supports, never leads.
4. **One blue, spent deliberately.** #0071e3 is the only chroma on the page. It lands on decisions (CTAs) and punctuation beats (the closing period). If blue appears somewhere that isn't a decision or a beat, it's diluted.
5. **Motion earns its keep.** One signature scroll moment per page; everything else is quiet, fast, exponential-ease reveals. A business owner at 9pm should never wait for an animation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast on the dark ink ground (paper text ≥ 4.5:1, gray secondary text checked against ink-deep). `prefers-reduced-motion` honoured globally — the CSS gate already kills animations, smooth scroll, marquee, and typing effects. Visible focus on every interactive element (global `:focus-visible` outline with pill-aware offsets). Touch targets ≥ 44px. Case study images carry descriptive alt text naming the client and system.
